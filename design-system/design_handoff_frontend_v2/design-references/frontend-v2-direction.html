Frontend v2 · direction, wireframes and specification · draft 01 · 08 Aug 2026
The build.

bathong.africa is live as a holding page. This document is the dictate for what replaces it: what we are learning from the houses that came before us, who we are building for, the eleven templates, and the exact rules for putting a photograph on a screen in South Africa. Nothing here invents a new visual language - it applies the one in this design system at full scale.

00 / where we are

A holding page and a promise

What is live today: a masthead, three doors labelled Build / Engage / Show, the dictionary card, a ticker, and a date - first walk, Saturday 29 August 2026. It does one job well, which is to say we exist and we are serious. It cannot do any of the jobs a collective actually needs: publish work, represent photographers, take submissions, sell membership, fill a walk, or put a show on a wall.

Keep

The three doors

Build / Engage / Show is a genuinely good structure. It is our navigation, not a homepage widget. Every page in v2 sits under one of those three.

Keep

The dictionary card

The definition is the strongest brand object we own. It moves to the About page and becomes the shareable 1080 asset it was designed to be.

Retire

"Launching soon"

The ticker survives; the coming-soon copy does not. From v2 the ticker carries live content - next walk, open photocall, latest essay.

01 / what we learn from

Seven houses, and what we take from each

These are studied for structure and behaviour, not for looks. We take the operating model and the reading experience. We take nothing of their visual identity - our identity is settled and is in this design system.

The line we hold

We are a collective, not an agency. Magnum, VII, NOOR and Panos all sell licences to editors; that is the business their sites are built around, and it is why their archives are search-first. We sell membership, teaching, walls and prints. Our site is reading-first. The archive comes in Phase 3 and serves curators and members, not stock buyers.

02 / user-centricity

Three people, three journeys

Every template below is justified by one of these three. If a page serves none of them it does not get built.

A · the photographer

Thabo, 24, Mamelodi

Shoots on a phone and a borrowed body. Found us on Instagram. Wants to know: is this for me, what does it cost, when is the next walk, will anyone actually look at my work.

Needs, in order: next walk date and whether it is free · what membership costs · how to submit to a photocall · proof that members get shown.

Failure mode: a site that reads as a gallery for people who already made it.

B · the viewer

Anyone who clicked a link

Arrived at one essay from a share. Has ninety seconds and probably 4G. Will either read to the end or leave.

Needs, in order: the first frame, fast · who made it · a way to keep going at the end.

Failure mode: a hero that loads for six seconds and a related-posts grid that shows nothing they want.

C · the institution

Curator, funder, editor, brand

Checking whether we are real before an email. Looking for governance, a body of work, and a named person to contact.

Needs, in order: who runs it · what has been shown and where · a photographer roster with contactable pages · NPC registration and reporting.

Failure mode: no About page with real names, no exhibition history, no way to reach a photographer directly.

The data question, which is a design question

Thabo is on a prepaid bundle. In South Africa a megabyte is a real cost to the person we most want to reach, and a photography site is the heaviest thing on the web. Every performance decision in section 06 is a user-centricity decision, not an engineering nicety. The budget: a homepage that costs under 1 MB on first view, an essay that streams frame by frame rather than loading twenty at once, and no autoplaying anything, ever.

03 / the unit

The photo essay is the atom. Everything else is packaging.

The brief already says it: 12 to 20 sequenced frames, edited as a body, published as a story. That is the thing we make. A walk produces frames; a group edit turns frames into an essay; an essay earns a wall; a wall makes a photographer. The site should make that pipeline visible, because the pipeline is the argument for joining.

Atom
Frame
one photograph + credit + caption
Molecule
Essay

Body
Photographer

Institution

Consequence for the build: the essay reader is the template we design first and best. If the reader is not excellent nothing else matters. The homepage is a shop window for the reader; the archive is an index of readers; the photographer page is a shelf of readers.

W1 · Home
Route / · paper → ink alternating
  1. Sticky nav. Three doors only. Paper at 88% with a 10px backdrop blur, as specified in the system. Join is an ink button, always present.
  2. One lead frame, 21/9, full bleed. Not a carousel. Not a slideshow. One photograph, changed weekly by editorial decision, credited in a mono capsule bottom-left. This is the whole first impression.
  3. The lead essay title sits under the photograph, not on it. Colour never goes over the work - system rule. Display type, uppercase, with the essay index.
  4. The three live blocks. Walk / photocall / membership, in that order, because that is Thabo's order of need. Jacaranda, signal and ghost - the only place three plates meet.
  5. Three recent essays. 3/2 frames, title and photographer below in mono. No excerpt text - the frame is the excerpt.
  6. The feed. Six single frames from members this week. This is the Everyday Africa lesson: the site must have a pulse between essays.

W2 · Essay reader
Route /stories/[slug] · ink ground · the hero template
  1. Reader chrome, not site chrome. The nav collapses to a wordmark, a frame counter that doubles as a progress rule, and a close. Everything else gets out of the way.
  2. Frames are sized to fit the viewport height, not the width. This is the single most important decision in the build. A 3/2 landscape frame at full browser width forces the viewer to scroll past a photograph to see it. We cap every frame at roughly 82vh so it is seen whole, as a print is seen whole.
  3. Caption capsule under every frame. Mono, index / place / time / credit. Opaque bar, hard edge, never a gradient over the image.
  4. Text is interleaved, not front-loaded. The photographer's words appear where the sequence needs them. Maximum 62 characters per line.
  5. The edit decides the layout. Two frames sit side by side when the edit pairs them, a single frame runs alone when it should breathe. This is why the template is modular - see the Magnum lesson.
  6. The end is a door, not a dead stop. Photographer credit with a link to their page, then the next essay. Never a grid of thumbnails.

W4 · Photographer page
Route /photographers/[name] · paper ground
  1. This page must survive being sent to a curator on its own. Portrait, name, member number, two-line bio, direct contact. It is a photographer's front page, hosted by us.
  2. A count row, not a badge system. Essays, frames, exhibitions, walks led. Real numbers or the row does not render - never a zero dressed up as a milestone.
  3. Essays first, singles second. The body of work outranks the highlights reel.
  4. The copyright line is a jacaranda plate, on the record. "You keep your copyright. Always." is a promise in the brief - here it is load-bearing UI, and it is what makes the page safe to send anywhere.

W5 · Walks & sessions
Route /walks · jacaranda lead on paper
  1. The next walk is the page. Date, time, meeting point, route, duration, what to bring, price, places left, one button. Everything a person needs to decide in eight seconds.
  2. Answer the fear, not the feature. The barrier to a first walk is not information, it is "am I good enough to come". Four lines that say no experience and no gear requirement do more work than any photograph.
  3. Ruled list for future walks with honest TBC dates, per the content rules. Never invent a date.
  4. Frames from the last walk close the page. The proof that showing up produces work.

W6 · Photocall
Route /photocalls · signal lead on paper
  1. Status, deadline and brief in one signal plate. Signal is the accent for the single most urgent thing on the site, and an open call is exactly that. Only one signal plate per page.
  2. The three lines that remove the excuse: anyone can enter, it costs nothing, you keep everything. These sit above the form, not in terms and conditions.
  3. The form is on the same screen as the brief. No "click here to apply" page. Fields are mono, 2px framed, per the system.
  4. Say what happens after. The written response frame by frame is our single strongest differentiator from every open call these photographers have entered before. It goes on the page, in public, as a promise.

Sizing, in principle

A photograph is seen whole or it is not seen

The most common failure on photography sites is a landscape frame set to 100% width on a 1600px monitor: the viewer scrolls through the middle of the picture and never sees the composition. Capping frame height at 82vh means the frame and its caption both sit within the viewport at the moment it arrives. Width follows from height and ratio, not the other way round.

Full bleed, when

Twice per essay at most

Full bleed is a device, not a layout. Reserve it for the opening frame and, if the edit calls for it, one turn in the middle. A full-bleed frame carries its caption below the fold of the image, in the standard capsule - never overlaid.

Delivery, in principle

Stream the essay, do not load it

Sixteen frames at 1440px is a 6 MB page if loaded naively. We serve frame 1 eagerly, frames 2 and 3 on idle, and everything else on approach. On a slow connection the reader still starts in under two seconds, which is the only number that matters.

07 / style specifics

What v2 adds to the system, and what it must not touch

Unchanged

The foundations hold

Four plates, three faces, radius 0, hard offset shadows, the 2px ink frame, two motion durations, inversion hovers, no camera iconography, credit on every frame. v2 changes no token. If a screen seems to need a fifth colour, the screen is wrong.

New · reader

Ink is the reading ground

The essay reader is the one template that runs ink-dominant end to end, because a dark ground is how photographs are looked at. Paper returns at the essay's end card. This is the only inversion of the paper 60 / ink 30 budget in the system, and it is deliberate.

New · progress

The counter is a rule

The reader's frame counter doubles as a 2px progress rule across the top - ink on paper, signal on ink. It is the only new graphic device v2 introduces, and it is made of the system's existing hairline.

New · density

The contact sheet run

EssayStrip already exists in the system as a numbered contact-sheet run. v2 promotes it to a primary layout on photographer pages and walk recaps, at 8 up desktop and 3 up mobile.

Watch

Signal discipline

Signal is 2% of the surface budget. With a photocall block on the homepage, the walks page and the photocall page, it is now the easiest token in the system to overuse. One signal element per page. If a page has an open call, that is the signal element.

Watch

The grain on photographs

The grain overlay sits on the page, at 0.5 opacity, over everything. On a dark reader with large frames this will read as noise on the photograph itself. Test at 0.3 in the reader, and be prepared to disable it over frames entirely. Real grain in the file beats fake grain on top of it.

Decisions I need from the group before hi-fi

1. Does the reader run on ink, as specified here, or on paper? This is the single biggest visual call in the build. 2. Is membership paid at launch or free for a founding cohort? It changes the homepage's third block entirely. 3. Do photographer pages carry direct contact details, or does licensing route through the collective? Magnum and NOOR route through the agency; we said photographers keep everything. 4. Who edits? The modular homepage assumes someone composes it weekly.
